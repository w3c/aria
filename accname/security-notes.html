The "aria-notify" permissions policy has a default allowlist of "*". This relaxes the Permission Policy default because requiring embedders to opt in for each frame would leave announcements coming from third-party content inaccessible by default. Cross-origin embedded content can invoke this user-facing channel without explicit delegation, which can cause uncontrolled disruption for uses of assistive technologies. Authors who want tighter control can still restrict the "aria-notify" policy explicitly, for example with <iframe allow="aria-notify 'none'"> or Permissions-Policy: aria-notify=(self).

The "aria-notify" permissions policy has a default allowlist of "*". This allows cross-origin embedded content to invoke ariaNotify() without explicit delegation from the embedding document. Choosing "*" supports accessibility announcements from third-party content without requiring embedders to enable the feature for each frame. However, it also allows untrusted embedded content to use this user-facing announcement channel. Repeated or misleading announcements may disrupt assistive technology users or interfere with their ability to understand and operate the page; malicious use may amount to denial of service against these users.

Embedding authors can restrict access explicitly, for example with <iframe allow="aria-notify 'none'"> or Permissions-Policy: aria-notify=(self). These restrictions control access to ariaNotify(); they do not prevent disruption through other announcement mechanisms, such as ARIA live regions. Allowing access to ariaNotify() does not itself provide protection against notification flooding.
